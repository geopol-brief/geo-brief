  ## What happened

  China's Foreign Ministry announced on 13 November that the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting would run in Shenzhen on 18-19 November, preceded by a CEO Summit on 17-18 November, with Xi Jinping chairing and giving the keynote. In the keynote on 18 November (full text published by Xinhua), Xi said China would cut tariffs and expand market access, "fully ensure" market access and an accommodating environment in manufacturing, steadily open services further, keep global industrial and supply chains stable, and uphold the WTO-centred system. The text gives no tariff rates, amounts or implementation dates, and export controls are not mentioned in it.

  The same day Putin criticised Western sanctions and Trump landed in Shenzhen after meetings in Manila; Xi received him at the airport, which the Moscow Times described as a first for any visiting leader since he became president. The Moscow Times and Just Security (citing Reuters) report a Trump-Xi-Putin meeting on the sidelines on Thursday 19 November. The Chinese Foreign Ministry's announcement did not name attendees, so the format is not confirmed by a primary source.

  ## Why it matters

  Beijing is positioning itself as the defender of open trade just as Washington tightens technology controls: Commerce widened licensing for advanced AI chips on 16 November, and China's commerce ministry warned of retaliation on 17 November. A Yahoo News preview puts the US-China truce deadline at 10 January and says Trump and Xi are expected to meet again at the G20 in Florida next month. The Moscow Times notes that China's annual trade surplus exceeds $1 trillion, a point Western partners criticise. Whether the speech's pledges become measurable tariff or access steps is not yet known.

  ## Implications for companies

  ### Supply chain
  Do not budget for lower Chinese tariffs until a notice from the State Council Tariff Commission or the Ministry of Commerce lists HS codes and dates. Keep the battery and graphite sourcing scenarios built around the 10 January pause date, since the keynote did not address export controls.

  ### Regulation
  Track whether a 19 November joint statement or fact sheet carries legal commitments on tariffs, rare earths or chips, and check licence conditions in contracts with Southeast Asian data-centre and electronics partners affected by the 16 November US rule.

  ### Market
  Manufacturers weighing China capacity or joint ventures should ask local partners which "manufacturing market access" measures would apply and when. Unspecific pledges are weak grounds for investment decisions; wait for implementing rules.

  ## What to watch next

  - 19 November: Trump-Xi-Putin meeting in Shenzhen (reported, agenda said to include Ukraine, Iran, trade, AI and nuclear weapons); closing of the APEC leaders' meeting
  - After 19 November: any joint statement, MOFCOM or Tariff Commission notice with HS codes and effective dates
  - 10 January: US-China truce and export-control pause deadline
  - December: expected Trump-Xi meeting at the G20 in Florida
