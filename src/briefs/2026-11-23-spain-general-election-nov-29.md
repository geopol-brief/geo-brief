  ## What happened

  Spain votes on Sunday 29 November 2026 for both chambers of the Cortes Generales. The Council of Ministers met in extraordinary session on 5 October, and Royal Decree 806/2026, published in the BOE on 6 October, dissolved the parliament elected in July 2023. The campaign ran for 15 days from 13 to 27 November, and the new Cortes is due to be constituted on 23 December at 10:00.

  The trigger was a parliamentary defeat on 2 October, when Congress rejected Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's two emergency housing decree-laws by 178-172 and 184-166, with Junts voting alongside the PP and Vox. Press accounts add housing protests, corruption cases involving the governing party's circle, and the resignation of Transport Minister Óscar Puente on 3 November after rail accidents.

  On polling, a tracker averaging 19 pollsters (data as of 17 November) shows the PP at 30.6%, the PSOE at 28.1%, Vox at 18.7% and Sumar-led left lists at 9.7%. Seat estimates put PP plus Vox at about 191 against a 176-seat majority in the 350-seat Congress. The picture is not uniform: the state pollster CIS (12 November) had the PSOE first at 33.3% to the PP's 25.5%, and the PP led in most but not all private polls. Publication of new polls is barred in the final five days, so today is the last day for fresh numbers.

  ## Why it matters

  Spain is a large euro-area economy and a major EU market for energy, autos and manufacturing. A PP-led government that depends on Vox would be the first time a far-right party takes cabinet roles since the end of the Franco era, according to press commentary, and could shift Madrid's line on climate, migration and EU files. Because the right needs Vox for a majority and the left is fragmented, the margin matters as much as the winner. Official results will be known on the night of 29 November, but coalition talks could stretch into the new year; no timetable has been announced and the outcome is not confirmed.
