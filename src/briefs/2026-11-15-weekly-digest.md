  ## What happened

  Four threads ran through the week. First, on 9 November China extended its suspension of export controls on some critical minerals and of a 24% additional tariff on US goods to 10 January 2027, while a 10% additional tariff stays; Washington extended 178 Section 301 exclusions. Second, on 5 November the US State Department widened Cuba-related sanctions to eight entities and two individuals, including two China-based firms accused of supplying Cuba's military and security services; Beijing protested. Third, on 13 November the White House said Trump, Xi and Putin plan a first three-way meeting on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen; the date is reported as Thursday 19 November, and officials stated agenda items (nuclear arsenals, Iran) but said Ukraine and chip export controls are not planned. Fourth, in the EU the first CBAM downstream trilogue (20 October) ended without a deal, and talks on the Critical Raw Materials Act are provisionally set to resume on 9 December.

  ## Why it matters

  The extension buys time but removes no measure: all pauses converge on 10 January 2027. The Cuba sanctions show that Washington is willing to list Chinese firms while the truce holds, and the trilateral shows that trade, nuclear and regional issues are now being bargained in the same week. A senior US official said no agreement is expected, so the meeting should be read as a signalling event rather than a source of binding commitments.

  ## Implications for companies

  ### Supply chain
  Keep the licensing-exposure list for battery materials, rare earths and graphite current and test it against 10 January 2027. Any scenario that assumes a durable deal before that date lacks support in the reporting.

  ### Regulation
  Screen counterparties against the latest US sanctions lists, including China-based suppliers, and check whether contracts allow suspension when a partner is listed. Track the CBAM downstream scope, which still has four open technical points including aluminium de minimis and scrap treatment.

  ### Market
  Expect headline-driven volatility around 19 November. Treat outcomes as unconfirmed until official readouts appear; the White House and Beijing may describe the same meeting differently.
