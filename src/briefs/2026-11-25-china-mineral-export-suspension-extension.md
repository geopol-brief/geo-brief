  ## What happened

  On 24 November 2026, several outlets (ANI, SCMP, Benzinga, MINING.COM) reported that China had extended by two months its suspension of select export restrictions on the United States, with critical minerals as the main subject and January 2027 given as the new horizon. Headlines tie the move to the Trump-Xi trade truce. We could not open the article bodies or the Ministry of Commerce announcement, so the exact items, the announcement number, the expiry date and whether licensing requirements remain are not confirmed.

  ## Why it matters

  A two-month extension, rather than a longer one, keeps the restrictions as a lever that can return in January. For manufacturers, the issue is less the current suspension than the repeated short deadlines: supply planning has to assume the curbs may come back.

  ## Implications for companies

  ### Supply chain
  List the Chinese-origin minerals, magnets and processed materials in your bill of materials and map each to a supplier, stock level and expected expiry. For battery and EV makers, check graphite, rare-earth magnet and related inputs first, once the covered items are confirmed.

  ### Regulation
  Check whether your contracts allow price or delivery adjustments if China's export licensing changes, and confirm that licences held for existing shipments stay valid under the extension.

  ### Market
  Expect price volatility in related minerals as January approaches. Review hedging and safety-stock policy with purchasing.

  ## What to watch next

  - The Ministry of Commerce text, to confirm items, number and exact expiry.
  - US-China talks ahead of the January deadline.
